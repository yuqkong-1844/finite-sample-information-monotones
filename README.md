# Finite-Sample Unbiasedly Estimable Information Monotones

This repository contains the Lean 4 formalization accompanying the paper
[*Finite-Sample Unbiasedly Estimable Information Monotones*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14225).
The machine-checked entry point is [`PaperProofs.lean`](PaperProofs.lean).

The formalization verifies the paper's principal rigidity and classification
results in all three dimension regimes. It also develops a reusable body of
determinantal algebra, including the theorem that every positive power of the
generic maximal-minor ideal over an arbitrary integral domain is primary.

## Motivation

Let $U=(u_{i\alpha})$ be the joint probability matrix of two finite random
variables. A functional of $U$ that is a polynomial of degree at most $d$
admits an unbiased estimator from $d$ independent samples. This motivates the
search for polynomial measures of dependence that also satisfy two basic
information-theoretic requirements:

1. **rank-one vanishing:** the functional is zero when the joint distribution
   is a product distribution; and
2. **data processing:** randomized post-processing of one variable cannot
   increase the functional.

The results formalized here show that these requirements are highly rigid. For
unequal alphabet sizes they force strong vanishing or determinantal-ideal
conditions; for equal alphabet sizes they force a determinant-square factor.
The formalization also verifies a low-degree Frobenius dependence functional
under a restricted channel class and the semantic reduction underlying the
paper's DPI-recognition hardness result.

## Setup and notation

The probability simplex is

$$
\Delta_{n,m}
=\{
U\in\mathbb R^{n\times m}:
u_{i\alpha}\ge 0,\quad \sum_{i,\alpha}u_{i\alpha}=1
\}.
$$

For an $n\times n$ column-stochastic matrix $T$, the formalized one-sided
data-processing inequality is

$$
F(TU)\le F(U),\qquad U\in\Delta_{n,m}.
$$

Rank-one vanishing is the condition

$$
U\in\Delta_{n,m},\quad \mathrm{rank}(U)\le 1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad F(U)=0.
$$

For a polynomial $P\in\mathbb R[u_{i\alpha}]$, write

$$
\tau=\sum_{i,\alpha}u_{i\alpha}.
$$

Thus the affine simplex is contained in the hyperplane $\tau=1$. In Lean,
rectangular matrices are represented by the definitionally equal iterated
function type `Fin n → Fin m → ℝ`; `Matrix.of` is used when matrix rank is
needed.

## Main results verified by Lean

Every result in this section is imported and checked by
[`PaperProofs.lean`](PaperProofs.lean).

### Rank-one vanishing bridge

The code initially uses an outer-product formulation of rank-one vanishing.
Lean proves that, on the probability simplex, this is equivalent to the
paper-facing rank-at-most-one formulation:

$$
\bigl(\forall x,y,\ F(xy^{\mathsf T})=0\bigr)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\bigl(\forall U,\ \mathrm{rank}(U)\le1\Rightarrow F(U)=0\bigr),
$$

where both quantifiers are restricted to points in the simplex.

```lean
GeneralAsymmetricC1.rko_iff_rankOneVanishing
```

The proof includes the algebraic facts that every outer product has rank at
most one and every real matrix of rank at most one is an outer product.

### Taller processed alphabet: $n>m\ge2$

Let $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^{n\times m}$ be open and contain
$\Delta_{n,m}$. If $F$ is $C^1$ on $\Omega$, satisfies left-sided DPI on
the simplex, and vanishes on simplex matrices of rank at most one, then

$$
F(U)=0\qquad\text{for every }U\in\Delta_{n,m}.
$$

The Lean theorem assumes `ContDiffOn ℝ 1 F Ω`; it does not assume that $F$
is globally $C^1$.

```lean
GeneralAsymmetricC1.general_asymmetric_simplex_C1_openNeighborhood
```

For a polynomial $P$, Lean derives the explicit simplex-ideal
factorization

$$
P=(\tau-1)Q
$$

for some polynomial $Q$.

```lean
AsymmetricPolynomial.asymmetric_polynomial_mem_simplex_ideal
```

### Equal alphabet sizes: $n=m\ge2$

If a polynomial $P$ satisfies left-sided DPI and rank-one vanishing on
$\Delta_{n,n}$, then there are polynomials $Q,K$ such that

$$
P=(\det U)^2Q+(\tau-1)K.
$$

```lean
SquarePolynomial.square_simplex_det_sq
```

Consequently, if $P$ is nonzero at some point of the simplex, then

$$
2n\le \mathrm{totalDegree}(P).
$$

```lean
SquarePolynomial.square_simplex_totalDegree_lower_bound
```

The square-case development also verifies constant compensation. If $P$ is
row-symmetric on the simplex, then there exists $M\ge0$ for which

$$
U\longmapsto (\det U)^2\bigl(P(U)+M\bigr)
$$

satisfies left-sided DPI. More explicitly, the formalization defines the
paper's normalized-defect supremum $M_*(P)$, proves that it is finite, and
proves that every $M\ge\max\{0,M_*(P)\}$ works.

```lean
SquarePolynomial.compensationRatioSet_bddAbove
SquarePolynomial.compensatedEval_dpi_of_compensationSup_le
SquarePolynomial.exists_constantCompensation_dpi
```

### Wider reported alphabet: $2\le n<m$

Let $I_n$ be the ideal generated by all maximal $n\times n$ minors of the
generic $n\times m$ matrix. If $P$ satisfies left-sided DPI and rank-one vanishing,
Lean proves

$$
P\in I_n^2+\langle\tau-1\rangle.
$$

```lean
WidePolynomial.wide_simplex_maximalMinor_sq
```

If $P$ is nonzero at some point of the simplex, Lean also proves

$$
2n\le \mathrm{totalDegree}(P).
$$

```lean
WidePolynomial.wide_simplex_totalDegree_lower_bound
```

### Frobenius Dependence Index

For $U\in\Delta_{n,m}$, let $r_i$ and $c_\alpha$ be its row and column
marginals. The formalized squared Frobenius dependence functional is

$$
F_{\mathrm{FDI}}(U)
=\sum_{i,\alpha}\bigl(u_{i\alpha}-r_ic_\alpha\bigr)^2.
$$

Lean proves that it vanishes exactly at rank-one simplex matrices:

$$
F_{\mathrm{FDI}}(U)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathrm{rank}(U)=1.
$$

It also proves two-sided DPI for every channel in the convex hull of the
doubly-stochastic channels and the rank-one erasure channels, and proves by an
exact rational $4\times3$ counterexample that unrestricted stochastic DPI
fails.

```lean
FrobeniusDependence.fdi_eq_zero_iff_rank_eq_one
FrobeniusDependence.restrictedChannel_iff_mem_convexHull
FrobeniusDependence.fdi_convexHull_dpi
FrobeniusDependence.fdi_not_full_dpi
```

### Semantic reduction from copositivity to DPI recognition

For a matrix $A$, the formalization constructs an explicit polynomial
functional of the form

$$
F_A(U)=(\det U)^2\,c(U)^{\mathsf T}A\,c(U)
$$

and proves, for $n\ge2$, that $F_A$ satisfies DPI if and only if $A$ is
copositive. The equivalence is also exported for rational input matrices. The
constructed family is proved row-symmetric and rank-one vanishing.

```lean
CopositiveDPIRecognition.rationalRecognitionPoly_dpi_iff_copositive
CopositiveDPIRecognition.recognitionPoly_rowSymmetricOnSimplex
CopositiveDPIRecognition.recognitionPoly_rankOneVanishing
CopositiveDPIRecognition.copositive_semantically_reduces_to_dpi
```

Lean verifies the complete mathematical and semantic reduction. The final
complexity-theoretic conclusion additionally uses the external theorem that
strong rational copositivity recognition is coNP-complete. That external
complexity theorem, together with a full formal model of polynomial-time
many-one reductions for rational arithmetic circuits, is not claimed as a
Lean theorem in this repository.

## Reusable determinantal algebra

The wide-case proof required a determinantal-algebra result that is useful
independently of probability simplices and information theory.

Let $D$ be an arbitrary integral domain, let $X$ be the generic
$p\times m$ matrix with $p\le m$, and let $I_p(X)$ be the ideal generated
by its maximal minors. Lean proves that

$$
I_p(X)\text{ is prime}
$$

and, for every positive integer $r$,

$$
I_p(X)^r\text{ is }I_p(X)\text{-primary}.
$$

In particular, the square of the generic maximal-minor ideal is primary. The
strong cancellation theorem used in the proof says

$$
a\notin I_p(X),\qquad ab\in I_p(X)^r
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
b\in I_p(X)^r.
$$

The coefficient-polymorphic declarations are:

```lean
GenericMaximalMinor.maximalMinorIdealOver_isPrime
GenericMaximalMinor.maximalMinorIdealOver_pow_isPrimary
GenericMaximalMinor.maximalMinorIdealOver_sq_isPrimary
GenericMaximalMinor.mem_maximalMinorIdealOver_pow_of_mul_mem_of_not_mem
```

Their specialization to the real wide-polynomial API, together with the
fixed-pivot saturation consequence, is:

```lean
WidePolynomial.maximalMinorIdeal_isPrime
WidePolynomial.maximalMinorIdeal_sq_isPrimary
WidePolynomial.mem_maximalMinorIdeal_sq_of_pivot_pow_mul_mem
```

The supporting development supplies additional reusable infrastructure:

- a two-minor straightening theorem over an arbitrary commutative ring;
- linear independence and spanning of standard products of nonempty minors;
- a standard-minor-product basis of the generic polynomial ring; and
- an exact weight/trailing-degree characterization of membership in every
  power of the maximal-minor ideal.

```lean
GenericMaximalMinor.two_minor_straightening
GenericMaximalMinor.standardMinorProducts_linearIndependent
GenericMaximalMinor.standardMinorProducts_span_eq_top
GenericMaximalMinor.standardMinorProductBasis
GenericMaximalMinor.mem_maximalMinorIdealOver_pow_iff_trailingDegree
```

The primaryness theorem is therefore not merely an assumption used by the
paper: its straightening, basis, filtration, cancellation, primality, and
primaryness arguments are all proved in Lean.

## Reproducing the verification

The project is pinned to Lean `v4.32.0` and mathlib `v4.32.0`. Install Lean
through [`elan`](https://lean-lang.org/install/), then run:

```bash
git clone https://github.com/yuqkong-1844/finite-sample-information-monotones.git
cd finite-sample-information-monotones
lake update
lake exe cache get
lake build
lake env lean PaperProofs.lean
```

The first two Lake commands obtain the pinned dependencies and the precompiled
mathlib cache. A successful verification ends with exit code `0`. The theorem
types and axiom reports printed by `PaperProofs.lean` are informational.

To inspect the proofs interactively, open the repository folder in VS Code with
the official **Lean 4** extension (`leanprover.lean4`), open
[`PaperProofs.lean`](PaperProofs.lean), and Cmd-click a declaration to jump to
its proof. Placing the cursor inside a tactic block displays the current goal
in the Lean Infoview.

`unsafe` shortcut. This can be checked with:

rg -n '\b(sorry|admit|axiom|unsafe)\b' --glob '*.lean' .
